# Demo script — Agua a Tiempo MX (3:30)

## 0:00–0:25 — Context
“This is Agua a Tiempo MX, a prototype for households managing intermittent water supply. It is not a live outage service, and every sample incident is marked as simulated.”

## 0:25–1:10 — Reserve ledger
“First I enter an estimated reserve in litres and its date. The number is a manual estimate, not a connected sensor. I can test the simulated sensor button; the UI explicitly says its value is fictional.”

## 1:10–1:50 — Delivery unit cost
“I enter estimated litres and what I paid. The product calculates MXN per litre only when both values are valid. It compares records; it does not verify a seller or water quality.”

## 1:50–2:25 — Incident card
“This card is labelled DEMO and not confirmed. It shows where a real pilot would need a source, accountable owner, status and next update. It never invents a restoration time.”

## 2:25–3:00 — Preparation and privacy
“I can mark what is possible for my household and view a privacy-safe summary. Data remains in this browser session; it is not sent to a server. A real service would need free notices and a non-smartphone route such as SMS or an agreed offline contact.”

## 3:00–3:30 — What changed my mind this week
“What changed my mind is that visibility is necessary but not enough. A household can know its reserve is low and still have no water. The hard part is not only measuring; it is getting verified information to people in time, with an accountable person responsible for corrections and follow-up. Until that handoff exists, the interface is a useful test—not a functioning public service.”
