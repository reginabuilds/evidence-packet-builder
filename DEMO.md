# DEMO SCRIPT — WEEK 08 · ESCUDO PyME

## 3-minute walkthrough + 30-second reflection

### 0:00–0:25 — Frame the problem

“ESCUDO PyME is not another antivirus. The problem I am testing is what happens when a small Mexican business has existing security tools but no internal cybersecurity team. The product turns those controls into clear priorities and creates a human response path when something goes wrong.”

Point to the **WEEK 08 · SIMULACIÓN** label and the synthetic-data notice.

### 0:25–1:05 — Show Mi Escudo

Open **Mi Escudo**.

Point to:
- the five prioritized controls;
- the plain-Spanish explanations;
- the status of each action;
- the simulated AI recommendation.

Say: “The AI can explain and prioritize, but it does not certify that the clinic is safe. Verification remains human or deterministic.”

### 1:05–1:55 — Simulate an incident

Open **Incidentes**.

Advance the five response steps.

Point to:
- severity;
- next responsible owner;
- preserve-evidence step;
- the boundary against attacker contact or ransom payment.

Say: “The system gives the team an order of operations, but a human coordinator remains responsible for context and irreversible decisions.”

### 1:55–2:30 — Show the coordinator

Open **Coordinador**.

Point to:
- synthetic incident ID;
- clinic owner;
- IT provider;
- next action;
- deadline;
- capacity for simultaneous incidents.

Say: “This is the operational layer: someone is named, the next action is visible, and escalation has a capacity limit.”

### 2:30–3:00 — Explain the decision boundary

Say: “We are not claiming that AI can manage every cyber crisis. The experiment is whether connecting existing controls to a simple workflow improves readiness and response for an SME without removing human verification.”

### 3:00–3:30 — What changed my mind

“I started this week with the idea that the opportunity might require another cybersecurity technology. The team discussion changed that. MFA, backups, monitoring and other controls already exist. The more defensible opportunity is the layer between those tools and the people who actually need to act. If the workflow does not improve continuity or response enough to justify itself, we should kill or redesign it.”
